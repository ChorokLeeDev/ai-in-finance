# Deep Switching State Space Model (DS3M)
**Xu, Peng & Chen (2021/2025)**
arXiv:2106.02329

## One-Paragraph Summary

DS3M combines recurrent neural networks with nonlinear switching state space models to simultaneously forecast time series and identify latent regimes. The model uses discrete latent variables (regimes) governed by a Markov chain and continuous latent variables for stochastic dynamics, with an RNN backbone (LSTM/GRU) capturing temporal patterns. Variational inference marginalizes over discrete states at O(KT) complexity. Evaluated on six real-world datasets (healthcare, economics, traffic, meteorology, energy), DS3M achieves state-of-the-art forecasting accuracy while producing interpretable regime assignments.

## Key Method: Deep Learning + Regime Switching

**Architecture (4-step generative process):**
1. Forward RNN processes inputs → hidden state h_t captures temporal patterns
2. Discrete regime d_t evolves via Markov chain (transition matrix Γ)
3. Continuous latent z_t transitions conditioned on both d_t and h_t
4. Observations y_t generated from z_t, h_t, and d_t

**Key innovations:**
- Skip connections linking RNN hidden state directly to observations
- Amortized variational inference with backward RNN for posterior
- Marginalization over discrete variables (avoids REINFORCE estimator)
- More stable regime identification than prior SNLDS methods

## Limitations

1. **Fixed regime count K**: Number of regimes must be pre-specified. Authors note: "It is challenging to choose the number of switching states. This is an open question in literature."
2. **No automatic state discovery**: Suggest Dirichlet priors for future work
3. **Purely predictive focus**: No causal interpretation of regimes or transitions
4. **Open-loop dynamics rejected**: Causes excessive regime switching in practice

## How Our Work Differs: CAUSAL DISCOVERY

| Aspect | DS3M (Xu et al.) | Our Work |
|--------|------------------|----------|
| **Primary goal** | Forecasting accuracy | Understanding WHY regimes change |
| **Regime interpretation** | Descriptive labels | Causal structure changes |
| **Cross-variable analysis** | Univariate focus | Multi-factor Granger causality |
| **Mechanism** | Correlational patterns | Structural breaks in causal links |
| **Actionability** | Predict what | Understand why + when |

**Key distinction:** DS3M identifies THAT regimes exist; we identify WHAT CHANGES between regimes (causal graph structure). Our Granger-HMM approach detects when cross-factor predictability decays, providing economically interpretable insights rather than black-box forecasts.
